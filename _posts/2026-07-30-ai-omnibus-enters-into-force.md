The EU's AI Omnibus officially entered into force on 27 July 2026.

This legislative update aims to simplify the administrative rules for using and developing AI across Europe. It introduces a more proportionate approach by extending many simplified obligations from small businesses to include small mid-cap companies. The update also provides extended timelines for high-risk AI systems, with some rules starting in 2027 and others in 2028. It expands access to regulatory sandboxes and introduces a new EU-level sandbox to help companies test their systems under supervision. Additionally, it clarifies the relationship between the AI Act and other existing EU laws to provide better legal certainty for developers.

For AI practitioners and public sector leaders, these changes are significant for balancing innovation with safety. By reducing the administrative burden, the Omnibus helps smaller European companies scale their AI technologies more easily while still meeting fundamental rights standards. The simplified AI literacy requirements mean that Member States and the Commission will take a larger role in education efforts. For those managing public service efficiency, the clearer procedures for conformity assessments and the expanded oversight by the AI Office provide a more predictable path for deployment. It ensures that safety remains a priority while removing some of the hurdles that often slow down technological growth.
